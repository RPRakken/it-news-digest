Microsoft says Windows Server 2016 will stop receiving security updates after it reaches the end of support in January 2027. "The January 2027 Windows security update will be the last update available for this version. After this date, devices running Windows Server 2016 will no longer receive monthly security updates containing protections from the latest security threats," Microsoft said in a message center update. According to Microsoft's lifecycle platform, it will reach the end of mainstream support in November 2029 and receive an additional five years of extended support until November 14, 2034. Those who want to test Windows Server 2025 before deployment can get a free 180-day Windows Server 2025 trial via the Microsoft Evaluation Center. More information about Windows servicing dates is available on the Windows Lifecycle FAQ page or via the Lifecycle Policy search tool. Advancing digital sovereignty with greater control and choice
blogs.microsoft.com • Oct 20, 2026

Across my conversations with leaders in government, critical infrastructure and regulated industries, one priority consistently rises to the top: putting technology to work on their most important systems while maintaining the control those systems require. This challenge is becoming more important as advanced cloud and AI capabilities move into public services, regulated processes and essential operations. Organizations are considering more than where data resides. They also seek to understand who can access and operate their systems, how sensitive digital assets remain protected, which technology dependencies matter most and whether critical services can continue as regulatory, geopolitical, security or network conditions change. Digital sovereignty should give organizations greater control and security over the technology they depend on, while continuing to innovate. Last year, we introduced Microsoft Sovereign Cloud to give customers more choice in how they address these requirements. Since then, we have continued to turn our commitments into practical capabilities across public cloud and customer-operated environments, while expanding how we enable partners to address sovereignty requirements in their local markets. Today, we are sharing the next stage of that work. A continuum of control, built around the workload Our approach to digital sovereignty starts with the workload. Each workload carries different requirements for data, operations, technology dependencies, continuity and risk. Microsoft Sovereign Cloud is designed to make it easier for organizations to apply the appropriate level of control to each workload without forcing every application into the same deployment model. Those needs can vary widely across organizations. A public-facing digital service may benefit from the scale and pace of innovation in the public cloud. A system supporting essential operations may need to continue running locally if connectivity is disrupted. An AI workload handling highly sensitive data may require models, data and processing to remain within a customer-controlled environment. Microsoft Sovereign Cloud is designed around this workload-based approach, providing a continuum of management, control and options across Sovereign Public Cloud, Sovereign Private Cloud and the Sovereign Partner Ecosystem. Organizations can apply the level of control appropriate to each workload while maintaining a consistent approach to governance and operations across their digital estate. Sovereign Public Cloud adds capabilities for workloads that require greater control and transparency while continuing to benefit from the scale and innovation of Azure. Sovereign Private Cloud extends cloud capabilities into customer-operated environments, including connected and disconnected operations. The Sovereign Partner Ecosystem broadens the continuum through independently operated environments, specialized partners, locally relevant expertise and technologies that support customers’ sovereignty requirements. These are not isolated destinations. Customers can combine public cloud, private cloud and partner capabilities across their digital estate, applying different levels of control to different workloads as their operating needs evolve. Making control more visible in Sovereign Public Cloud For many workloads, the public cloud provides a combination of scale, innovation, integrated security and compliance. Organizations can benefit from these capabilities while applying additional sovereignty controls for residency, confidentiality, operator access, policy and continuity. Our work in Sovereign Public Cloud makes these controls easier to understand, apply and demonstrate. Sovereign Control Panel, entering public preview at Microsoft Ignite, brings these signals together in a unified Azure portal experience. It helps organizations understand their sovereignty posture, apply and manage controls, identify gaps and risks and take action to address them.

Require Google Workspace authentication on Android Enterprise devices with Microsoft Intune
techcommunity.microsoft.com • Oct 19, 2026

By: Samuel Vara, Software Engineer &amp; Mayur Jadhav, Product Manager | Microsoft Intune We’re excited to announce that organizations using Android Enterprise can now require users on managed devices to authenticate with a managed Google Account. This capability is generally available in Microsoft Intune and helps organizations use their Google Workspace identities as the primary management identity on supported Android Enterprise devices. The new policy supports organizations that have upgraded their Managed Google Play enterprise to a managed Google domain enterprise. Use managed Google Accounts on enrolled devices &nbsp;Android Enterprise has traditionally used managed Google Play accounts solely to enroll and manage devices and distribute corporate apps, rather than as user identities for accessing Google services outside the managed device. &nbsp;With this policy, admins can now require users to enroll and authenticate with their organization’s managed Google Workspace Account instead of using the device’s managed Google Play account. This gives users access to Google apps and services with their organizational identity. This feature doesn‘t impact the Microsoft Entra account associated with the managed device. This capability supports the following Android Enterprise enrollment types: Corporate-owned, fully managed devices Corporate-owned devices with a work profile Note: Corporate-owned dedicated devices aren't supported because they aren't associated with an individual user. Prepare your environment Before you configure the policy, complete these prerequisites: Verify your organization's Google Workspace domain. Admins can verify an organization's domain on the Google Admin console by following the instructions from Verify your domain for Google Workspace&nbsp;|&nbsp;Domain management&nbsp;|&nbsp;Google Workspace Help Upgrade the Managed Google Play enterprise to a managed Google domain enterprise, if required, using the Upgrade button on the Intune admin center. Customers can follow the instructions in the blog&nbsp; New onboarding flow to managing Android Enterprise devices with Microsoft Intune to upgrade or onboard their tenant. Note that the admin performing the upgrade must have "Super Admin" rolein Google Workspace during the upgrade process. More instruction on how to assign such permission to a user can be found in Prebuilt administrator roles&nbsp;| User management&nbsp;|&nbsp;Google Workspace Help If users don't already have managed Google Accounts created in the enterprise's Google Workspace domain, create them following the instructions in the "Domain verified" section of Add an account for a new user&nbsp;|&nbsp;User management |&nbsp;Google Workspace Help Use an account with at least the Policy and Profile Manager role in&nbsp; Intune for the above oprations within the Intune admin center. Configure the policy in Microsoft Intune In the Intune admin center, Create a policy using settings catalog in Microsoft Intune or follow the instructions below: Go to Devices &gt; Manage devices &gt; Configuration &gt; Create &gt; New policy. For Platform, select Android Enterprise. For Profile type, select Settings catalog. In Configuration settings, open Device restrictions &gt; Users and Accounts. Add Require users to authenticate with a Google Workspace account and set it to True. ssign the policy to groups containing users that want to use the managed Google accounts for authentication and enrollment on their Intune managed Android devices. Review and create the policy. You can assign the policy before enrollment or to devices that are already enrolled. If you assign it before enrollment, the authentication prompt appears after enrollment. On an enrolled device, the prompt appears after the device receives the policy. What users experience After the policy reaches the device, the user receives a Sign in with your work account prompt on the device. The user clicks on the prompt and adds a managed Google Account from the organization's enterprise domain and completes authentication. Until the user completes the prompt and authenticates with the Google Account, the policy setting reports an "Error" state, and the Gmail app, if present on the device, is disabled. This policy's non-compliance doesn't change the device's overall compliance state. Once a Google Account is chosen the user completes authentication, the managed Google Play account is replaced on the device by the chosen Google Account.Figure 01: Android phone lock screen showing a Device Policy notification prompting the user to "Sign in with a work account". Verify deployment Administrators can monitor the policy from Devices &gt; Manage devices &gt; Configuration &gt; Policies in the Intune admin center. Select the policy to review device and user check-in status, device assignment status, and per-setting status. After the user completes authentication, confirm that the managed Google Account is available on the device. Conclusion This new Intune capability gives organizations greater control over how users access Google apps and services on supported corporate-owned Android Enterprise devices. By requiring authentication with a managed Google Account from the organization’s Google Workspace domain, admins can provide a consistent organizational identity and give users access to the Google Workspace resources and services assigned to that account, while retaining Microsoft Entra as the device’s management identity. Review the prerequisites, configure the settings catalog policy, and monitor deployment to bring this experience to your users.

BlackX UEFI Bootkit Bypasses Secure Boot on Patched Windows Systems Missing Revocations
thehackernews.com • Oct 24, 2026

Diplomatic organizations in Central Asia have been targeted by a sophisticated new Unified Extensible Firmware Interface (UEFI) bootkit called BlackX. The activity, which singled out diplomatic entities in Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, and Turkmenistan in early 2026, is suspected to be orchestrated by a China-linked state-sponsored hacking group known as APT41, Kaspersky said in a technical report published this week. The primary objective behind deploying the malware is to undermine Microsoft Windows startup defenses, along with third-party security solutions, while maintaining long-term, covert access to compromised devices. Notably, BlackX is assessed to be an updated version of BlackLotus, a notorious bootkit that was first observed being advertised on cybercrime forums in late 2022, before its existence was officially confirmed by ESET in March 2023. BlackLotus is designed to exploit a now-patched Secure Boot bypass vulnerability tracked as CVE-2022-21894 (CVSS score: 4.4), also known as Baton Drop, to load malicious code before the operating system starts. However, successful exploitation hinges on an attacker having administrator privileges or physical access to a target host. "It is critical to note that a threat actor's use of this bootkit is primarily a persistence and defense evasion mechanism," Microsoft noted at the time. "It is not a first-stage payload or an initial access vector and can only be deployed to a device to which a threat actor has already gained either privileged access or physical access." That said, completely addressing the threat requires additional actions beyond installing the latest update, including running manual steps to apply Microsoft's Secure Boot revocation mitigations, such as adding the "Windows Production PCA 2011" certificate to the Secure Boot UEFI Forbidden List (DBX). The Russian cybersecurity company has found that attackers are capitalizing on the fact that organizations have not carried out these manual steps, allowing them to continue using the Baton Drop vulnerability even on systems where the "patch" has been applied. "On October 19, 2026, this certificate expired, but this does not prevent the vulnerable binaries from loading unless the certificate is blocklisted," researchers Georgy Kucherin, Vladimir Nestor, and Vasily Berdnikov said. "It is likely that the threat actor behind this campaign took into account that few organizations realized the importance of manually activating the Baton Drop patch. As such, we believe they decided to invest time in understanding how BlackLotus works and creating a copycat of this bootkit." Attackers have been observed deploying BlackX after breaching victim networks through an unspecified method and gaining administrator privileges. To install the bootkit, the threat actor drops the following files - - inst.exe, an installer executable - PVMDIJGKAX2, an encrypted extensionless file containing files and strings required for the installation The installer is then launched via the command line as "C:\ProgramData\Intel\inst.exe PVMDIJGKAX2 Q46F6MDLI3RX2KV1," where "Q46F6MDLI3RX2KV1" is the RC4 key used for decryption. The binary then drops additional files to both system and UEFI partitions to exploit CVE-2022-21894 in a manner that's "remarkably similar" to the one used in BlackLotus.

Arista Warns of Critical VeloCloud SQL Injection Zero-Day Exploited in Attacks
thehackernews.com • Oct 23, 2026

Attackers are exploiting a zero-day SQL injection flaw in Arista's VeloCloud Orchestrator (VCO), the central management system for VeloCloud SD-WAN networks, the company said in an advisory on October 22. The flaw lets anyone who can log in to the orchestrator, even with a read-only customer account, gain operator privileges, the top level of control over it. For on-premises orchestrators, the fix is in VCO 5.2.3.18, 6.1.3.10, and 6.4.2.12, while Hosted and Dedicated orchestrators have already been patched. The flaw is tracked as CVE-2026-114625 and rated 9.9 on the CVSS scale. Orchestrators running the releases that fixed the VCO flaws exploited in July and September are still affected, because those releases are older than the new fixes. Arista bought the VeloCloud SD-WAN product line from Broadcom, announcing the deal in July 2025. The products were earlier known as VMware SD-WAN by VeloCloud. To exploit the flaw, an attacker needs network access to the orchestrator's web interface and a valid login. The minimum role is Enterprise Read Only, the lowest built-in role for customer users. VCO is exposed by default, and no VCO setting prevents the exposure. In VCO, operators create the customer and partner accounts, and each partner can manage a group of customers. The flaw allows an attacker to act as a local superuser, a role with full access. It may also allow an attacker to affect the VCO host itself and expose, modify, or disrupt the orchestrator and the data it manages. The advisory does not say when the attacks began, which orchestrators were hit, or what the attackers did after exploiting the flaw. The new flaw is the third in VCO since late July that Arista has reported as exploited, and the first of the three that requires a login. Neither the July flaw (CVE-2026-16812) nor the September flaw (CVE-2026-93952) needed one. Affected and Fixed Releases Each affected release train has its own fix, shown here beside the release that fixed September's flaw on the same train. VeloCloud Gateway and VeloCloud Edge are not affected. The 7.0 release train is not on the list, and Arista says any release not on the list is not vulnerable. Arista has not checked releases that have reached end of support. Customers on an unsupported release train can contact Arista's Technical Assistance Center (TAC) about upgrade options. As of October 22, September's flaw is fixed only in the 5.2 and 6.4 trains. On 6.1, it affects releases up to 6.1.3.7, and no fixed release is listed. Neither advisory says whether 6.1.3.10 also fixes the September flaw. If You Cannot Upgrade Yet Until the fixed release is installed, Arista recommends these steps: - Limit access to the VCO web interface to trusted administrative networks. - Monitor the VCO for connections from known malicious IP addresses. - Watch for unexpected outbound network activity from the VCO host. - Consider blocking outbound ports that normal operation does not need. - Watch for backdoor daemons and webshells. - Review recent administrator activity for unexpected changes. Checking for Compromise Two IP addresses have been seen carrying out the attacks, according to Arista. Block them and check logs for any sign of them: - IP: 74.82.197[.]86 - IP: 38.54.50[.]151 Also check VCO web access logs for requests with unusual URL-like path components, encoded characters, references to local or internal services, or high request rates. If you suspect a compromise, save evidence before fixing anything, where possible. That means the VCO web access, backend application, system and database logs, along with the relevant file-system timestamps. Regarding the July and September flaws, Arista warned that a compromised orchestrator could also grant attackers access to VeloCloud Edge devices. For this flaw, the advisory does not say whether the risk applies or whether upgrading removes access that an attacker has already gained.

Attackers Abuse UpdraftCentral Keys to Plant Backdoors on WordPress Sites
thehackernews.com • Oct 21, 2026

Unknown threat actors breached TeamUpdraft's website and potentially gained unauthorized access to customers' connection keys for UpdraftCentral, UpdraftVault, and UpdraftClone, using them to backdoor WordPress sites. TeamUpdraft is the developer behind UpdraftPlus, a popular WordPress backup plugin with over four million active installations. The vendor also offers UpdraftCentral, a remote management service that's used for managing multiple sites remotely. A client component of UpdraftCentral is shipped within every copy of UpdraftPlus. "Over the weekend of 17-18 October we identified unauthorized access to the teamupdraft.com website," TeamUpdraft said in a statement. "Our team responded straight away and worked through the night to contain it – we closed the access route, signed out every account and replaced the keys and credentials that could have been affected." As a result of the incident, threat actors are believed to have accessed its customer database, which holds the following information - - Names, email addresses, billing addresses and order history - Account passwords, stored only in hashed (scrambled) form and never in plain text - Two-factor authentication (2FA) settings - Connection keys for UpdraftCentral, UpdraftVault and UpdraftClone TeamUpdraft said the database does not hold any financial information, and that they are handled by Stripe and PayPal.
